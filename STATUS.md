## Method correction: human cognition transfer

Do **not** kill a question because the human phenomenon is old.

A mature human theory can provide:
- the scientific object;
- competing hypotheses;
- clean experimental manipulations;
- a human reference curve.

The LLM/NLP novelty must come from what happens after transfer.

Strong outcomes include:
- model-family / training-regime split;
- LLM/human structural divergence;
- systematic non-human error law;
- explicit-reasoning vs downstream-use dissociation;
- representation/behavior dissociation;
- new causal computation.

Weak outcome:

> “Humans show X; LLMs also show X.”

That alone normally does not establish the desired Main-level distinction.

## CK correction

2026 PNAS work on recursive mentalizing + public salience **does not kill CK**.

It becomes a calibration anchor.

CK asks whether, with first-order facts held fixed, public observability changes LLM recursive
knowledge computation from ordinary finite nested ToM into a compressed common-knowledge state.

The pilot must search for an **LLM-specific law**, not merely reproduce the human publicness effect.

## New serious leads

### EPM — perspective-relative epistemic modality

Core question:

> Does `might/must` get evaluated against the relevant agent's information state, or does
> narrator/model-global truth leak into the modal judgment?

This isolates a missing interaction between:
- modal semantics;
- agent-indexed information state / ToM.

No pilot until dedicated assassination finishes.

### HWP — hypothetical-world persistence

Core question:

> Once language opens a hypothetical world, can the model keep that branch distinct and
> update it persistently without hypothetical→actual or actual→hypothetical leakage?

This uses modal subordination as a clean linguistic identification tool for a broader
hypothetical-world-state question.

No pilot until counterfactual/world-state literature assassination finishes.

### FC — free-choice inference

Classic `may(A or B)` free-choice structure is still theoretically active.

A simple accuracy benchmark is not enough. The only serious version is whether models show a
coherent structural regime across free-choice, negation/dual-prohibition and related modal
inferences.

No pilot yet.
